\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}}

Let $D_c=\thetahat_n(c)-\theta(c)$ and $X_i = \varphi(c;Z_i)-\theta(c)$, so that
$D_c=n^{-1}\sum_i X_i$, $\Ex X_i =0$, $|X_i|\le 1$ and $\Var X_i\le\sigma^2_{\max}$.
Bernstein's moment-generating-function bound for a centred variable with
$|X|\le b$ gives $\log\Ex e^{\lambda X}\le\lambda^2\Var(X)/(2(1-\lambda b/3))$ for
$0<\lambda<3/b$; summing over $n$ independent copies and rescaling by $1/n$,
\[
\log\Ex e^{\lambda D_c}\;\le\;\frac{\lambda^2 v}{2(1-\kappa\lambda)},
\qquad v=\frac{\sigma^2_{\max}}{n},\quad \kappa=\frac{1}{3n},
\]
which is exactly the statement that $D_c$ is \emph{sub-gamma on the right tail}
with variance factor $v$ and scale parameter $\kappa$, in the sense of
\citet[\S2.4]{boucheron2013concentration}. Their maximal inequality
\citep[Cor.~2.6, p.~33]{boucheron2013concentration} --- which requires the
variables to be centred but \emph{not} independent of one another, so the strong
correlation between circuit hypotheses is harmless --- then gives
\[
\Ex\max_{c\in\Cclass} D_c \;\le\; \sqrt{2v\log m} + \kappa\log m
\;=\; \sigma_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{2\log m}{n}} + \frac{\log m}{3n}.
\]
Finally $\thetahat_n(\hchat)-\theta(\hchat)\le\max_c D_c$ pointwise for any
selection rule, so the same bound holds for its expectation. \hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:lower}}

Under the stated model $\sqrt n(\thetahat_n(c)-\theta_0)/\sigma = N_c$ with
$N_1,\dots,N_m$ i.i.d.\ standard normal, and the arg-max selects the largest
$N_c$, so $\thetahat_n(\hchat)-\theta(\hchat)=(\sigma/\sqrt n)\max_c N_c$
exactly. For i.i.d.\ standard normals,
$\Ex\max_{j\le m}N_j\ge \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\sqrt{\log m}$
\citep[Exercise~13.6]{boucheron2013concentration}, used there in the proof of
Sudakov's minoration; combined with the sub-Gaussian upper bound
$\Ex\max_{j\le m}N_j\le\sqrt{2\log m}$ this gives the claim. \hfill$\square$

\begin{remark}[The restriction to $m\ge3$]
The bound with constant $1/\sqrt2$ fails at $m=2$, where
$\Ex\max_{j\le 2}N_j = 1/\sqrt\pi = 0.5642$ exactly while
$\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\sqrt{\log 2}=0.5887$. The ratio
$\Ex[\max_{j\le m}N_j]/\sqrt{\log m}$ is minimised at $m=2$ and increases
thereafter, so the largest constant valid for every $m\ge2$ is
$1/\sqrt{\pi\log 2}\approx0.6777$; we computed the ratio numerically up to
$m=10^5$ ($0.678$ at $m=2$, $0.807$ at $m=3$, $0.874$ at $m=4$, $1.269$ at
$m=10^4$). Nothing in the paper depends on the case $m=2$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:naivecov}}

The naive bound at $\hchat$ is
$\thetahat_n(\hchat)-z_{1-\alpha}\sigmahat(\hchat)/\sqrt n$, and (treating
$\sigmahat$ as $\sigma$, which is exact in the Gaussian limit) it covers iff
$G_{\hchat}\le z_{1-\alpha}$. Since $\hchat=\argmax_c\thetahat_n(c)$ and all
$\theta(c)$ and $\sigma(c)$ are equal, $\hchat=\argmax_c G_c$, so the coverage
event is exactly $\{\max_c G_c\le z_{1-\alpha}\}$. Writing the equicorrelated
Gaussian vector as $G_c=\sqrt\rho\,W+\sqrt{1-\rho}\,\varepsilon_c$ with
$W,\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_m$ i.i.d.\ standard normal and conditioning on
$W$ makes the coordinates independent, giving the stated integral.
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Validity of the bounds in Section~\ref{sec:methods}}

\paragraph{(a) and (b).} Both are unions of the Maurer--Pontil bound
\citep[Thm.~4]{maurer2009empirical}, applied at level $\alpha/m$ and
$\alpha\pi(c)$ respectively. In case (b), $\sum_c \alpha\pi(c)\le\alpha$ because
$\pi$ is a sub-probability, so the union bound applies. Both statements are
\emph{simultaneous} over $\Cclass$, hence hold at the (random) $\hchat$:
\[
\Prb\big(\theta(\hchat)<L(\hchat)\big)\le\Prb\big(\exists c:\theta(c)<L(c)\big)\le\alpha .
\]
Crucially, this argument uses nothing about how $\hchat$ was chosen, so it covers
greedy search, gradient-based alignment search, and human inspection alike.

\paragraph{(c).} Write $x_{ic}=\varphi(c;Z_i)-\theta(c)$. For the
\emph{non-studentised} maximum $\max_c n^{-1/2}\sum_i x_{ic}$,
\citet[Thm.~3.2 and Cor.~3.1]{chernozhukov2013gaussian} show that the Gaussian
multiplier bootstrap quantile satisfies
$\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}|\Prb(T\le c_W(\alpha))-\alpha|\le Cn^{-c}$ provided
(i) $c_1\le n^{-1}\sum_i\Ex[x_{ic}^2]\le C_1$ for every $c$ and
(ii) $B_n^2(\log(mn))^7/n\le C_2 n^{-c_2}$, where $B_n$ is an envelope constant.
Our scores are bounded in $[0,1]$, so $B_n=1$ and condition (ii) reduces to
$(\log(mn))^7/n\le C_2n^{-c_2}$, i.e.\ $\log m$ may grow almost as fast as
$n^{1/7}$. The band we actually use is \emph{studentised} and is built from the
\emph{empirical} (resampling) bootstrap with the standard deviation recomputed
in each replicate; validity for exactly this construction --- the studentised
maximum with either multiplier or empirical bootstrap critical values --- is
\citet[Thm.~4.3, ``Validity of one-step MB and EB methods'']{cck2019moment},
whose Comment~4.6 describes the result as the studentised counterpart of the
2013 theorem. Condition~(i) is the one that
bites in practice: a hypothesis scoring exactly $1$ on every instance has
$\sigmahat(c)=0$. We floor $\sigmahat$ at $10^{-4}$; in every experiment reported
here we also verify coverage empirically, which is the ultimate check.

\paragraph{(e).} The conditional interval is the polyhedral construction of
\citet{lee2016exact}; our implementation restricts the polyhedron to the
comparisons the investigator actually made (so that a size-constrained arg-max is
handled correctly). The hybrid intersects the conditional interval with a
two-sided simultaneous band at level $\beta$ and then inverts at level
$(\alpha-\beta)/(1-\beta)$; because we use a simultaneous band rather than the
exact projection region of \citet{andrews2024inference}, the result is
conservative.

\paragraph{(d).} Conditionally on the selection half, $\hchat$ is fixed and the
evaluation half is an i.i.d.\ sample independent of it, so the $m=1$ bound
applies conditionally and therefore marginally.

\section{The ratio metric}
\label{app:ratio}

Write $a_c=\Ex[\mathrm{LD}(c)]$, $b=\Ex[\mathrm{LD}(\emptyset)]$,
$f=\Ex[\mathrm{LD}(\text{full})]$ and $F(c)=(a_c-b)/(f-b)$. The map
$(a,b,f)\mapsto(a-b)/(f-b)$ is differentiable wherever $f\ne b$, with gradient
\[
\Big(\tfrac{1}{f-b},\ \tfrac{-1}{f-b}+\tfrac{a-b}{(f-b)^2},\ \tfrac{-(a-b)}{(f-b)^2}\Big).
\]
Substituting the empirical means and multiplying by the centred per-instance
vector gives the influence contribution quoted in Section~\ref{sec:ratio}:
\[
\psi_i(c)=\frac{\big(\mathrm{LD}_i(c)-\hat a_c\big)-\big(\mathrm{LD}_i(\emptyset)-\hat b\big)}{\hat f-\hat b}
-\hat F(c)\,\frac{\big(\mathrm{LD}_i(\text{full})-\hat f\big)-\big(\mathrm{LD}_i(\emptyset)-\hat b\big)}{\hat f-\hat b}.
\]
The delta method gives $\hat F(c)-F(c)=n^{-1}\sum_i\psi_i(c)+o_P(n^{-1/2})$
uniformly over $c$ under bounded fourth moments. The band we report resamples
the instances and recomputes $F$, its influence function and its standard error
in every replicate --- the ratio analogue of the bootstrap-$t$ of
Section~\ref{sec:methods}(c). Because
$\psi_i(c) = \big(u_i(c) - F(c)\,v_i\big)/\bar v$ with
$u_i(c)=\mathrm{LD}_i(c)-\mathrm{LD}_i(\emptyset)$ and
$v_i=\mathrm{LD}_i(\text{full})-\mathrm{LD}_i(\emptyset)$, each replicate needs
only three weighted sums of the $n\times m$ matrices $u$, $u^2$ and $uv$, so the
whole band costs three mat-muls per batch of replicates.

\paragraph{A finite-sample alternative.}
If the per-instance logit differences are known to lie in $[\ell,u]$, one can
build two-sided empirical-Bernstein intervals for $a_c$ (simultaneously over $c$,
at level $\alpha/3$), for $b$ and for $f$ (each at level $\alpha/3$), and then
report the worst-case value of $(a-b)/(f-b)$ over the resulting box. The result
is a finite-sample, simultaneous, post-selection-valid bound; it is considerably
more conservative than the bootstrap band, but it does not rely on asymptotics.
This is implemented as \texttt{psf.functional.ratio\_union\_lcb}.
